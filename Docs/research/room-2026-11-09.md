# The recording room

9 November 2026, Keyra 0.4.27. Every recording the engine is calibrated on
was taken in a room, and between a strike's partials each holds that room's
reverberation. 0.4.26 played none: between the partials it had only the
board's modes and the noise bands, which fall with the strike. 0.4.27 plays
each piano's room, measured from its train takes, through a feedback delay
network that hears the strike's noise and the board's ring.

The measurement is `Tools/fit_room.py`; its protocol is at the head of the
tool and its receipt is [`room-2026-11-09.json`](room-2026-11-09.json), which
`Source/DSP/MeasuredRoom.h` is generated from and `Keyra.MeasuredRoom`
checks. It reads the train takes of each piano's locked split only. No take
is loaded or distributed, and the room is fitted numbers, not a recorded
impulse response.

## What the recordings hold between the partials

In 100 ms windows from 0.1 to 0.6 s after the strike, per third octave the
floor between the partials (the median power of the bins 75 Hz clear of
every partial the recording holds, over ln 2) falls at one rate whatever the
note, and above 250 Hz the two microphones hear it uncorrelated. 0.4.26's
floor fell faster and stood under it by 0.45 s. Over each piano's train takes,
the median of the engine's floor less the recording's at 0.15 and at 0.45 s,

and 0.4.27's:

## What the room hears

The fitted partials are measured from the same recordings, so each holds the
room at its own frequency already: its level, its decay and its beating are
the room's as much as the string's. A room fed the partials counts that
twice. What the partials leave out is the strike's broadband sound, which the
engine plays as the board's ring (its modes to 1 kHz) and the noise bands
above, and the room hears those, the pair's mid less every partial, phantom,
undamped string and beat.

Two offline prototypes added a statistical room (decorrelated noise falling
at each third octave's measured rate, its level per band fitted for its own
input) to 0.4.26's renders, scored on the benchmark before this round's fix
to its band scoring. Summed over the eleven measures other than level
in tolerance units, train and dev, fed the whole output it stood the grand's
takes further (+3.75, +3.19) and the Hamburg's (+5.12, +4.03); fed only the
noise and the ring it brought all five pianos closer (the grand −2.28, −1.75;
the upright −2.71, −2.71; the Hamamatsu −0.66, −0.49; the Hamburg −8.13,
−0.25; New York −2.14, −3.65). The engine plays the second.

## The network

The room plays as a feedback delay network (J.-M. Jot and A. Chaigne,
"Digital delay networks for designing artificial reverberators", AES 90th
Convention, 1991; `KeyraEngine.cpp`): sixteen lines of 11.3 to 67.5 ms, each
the first prime at or above its length in samples at the engine's rate, so
that no two lines' echoes coincide; the 16-point Hadamard matrix mixes their
outputs back into them; each line loses the room's decay over one pass
through a first-order shelf, the low rate at DC and the high one at Nyquist;
the input enters each line with a fixed sign, and the left and right outputs are
two rows of the matrix. Their correlation over a unit impulse's first 2 s is
0.013. The lines' lengths and losses scale with the rate, so a band's decay
and its energy per hertz do not depend on it (`Keyra.Room`: within 10 % of
the shelf's rate at 250 Hz to 12 kHz and within 0.5 dB at 250 Hz and 2 kHz,
at 44.1, 48 and 96 kHz).

A build between pianos plays a room between theirs, each of its four numbers
the soundboards' shares' geometric mean; a build change while the room rings
glides its losses over 10 ms and ends on the new room exactly. The room
sleeps when its energy and its input stay under 10⁻²⁴ for longer than its
longest line, and a strike after any silence plays exactly as after a
reset. `keyra::Parameters::room` scales what enters it: 0 plays none, 1 the
measured room, 2 twice; no front end exposes it yet, and the plug-in plays it
as measured.

## Measuring each room

Per band the rate is a line through the frames 15 dB over the take's own
floor, from 1.5 times the note's fundamental up, the median over five takes
at least; the network's shelf is fitted to the measured bands, the crossover
the one of eight from 250 Hz to 6.3 kHz with the least error; the gain is
the one that brings the engine's floor to the recording's, the median over
the bands whose rates were measured, where the floor decays as the room
does. Each take is rendered at its layer's velocity through the engine
without its room and with the room alone at unit gain, both scaled to the
recording as the benchmark scores them.

scale, so it compares rooms only alongside the engine's floor for each piano.
Three runs of the fit, on renderers built with and without these numbers in
the header, wrote the same header.

## What the room does not do

- One gain per piano. The board's modes to 1 kHz and the noise bands above
  were measured from the recordings, whose floor holds the room's first
  part. Where they already stood at the recordings' floor, between 250 Hz and
  1 kHz, the room takes the engine's floor over it by 0.45 s: the
  Hamamatsu's by 5.6 dB and New York's by 7.2 dB on their train takes, the
  grand's by 1.0 and the Hamburg's by 0.5. Measuring the board and the noise
  again with the room in place would let each hold only its own part. A
  level per band fitted to what the render lacks would close it on the
  benchmark, as the statistical prototype's did, but outside the bands
  where the floor decays as a room it would stand in for whatever else the
  engine leaves out there, such as the strike's low thud (README, Known
  gaps), so the room keeps one gain.
- Under 250 Hz the four grands' floors are correlated between their
  microphones (+0.77 to +0.93 at 0.15 s), and the upright's Blumlein pair
  hears it at −0.58. The room's channels are uncorrelated at every
  frequency, so it takes the engine's low correlation under the grand's
  recordings (0.71 against 0.89) and the Hamburg's (0.37 against 0.77);
  the Hamburg's four coherence scores past a tolerance are probably this.
- The network starts diffuse: its first echo comes 11.3 ms after the strike
  and no early reflection of the recordings' rooms (the floor, the lid, the
  nearest walls) is modelled.
- A first-order shelf per line holds two rates. The upright's measured rates
  spread from 29 to 86 dB/s and the shelf follows them within 8.7 dB/s rms;
  the other pianos' within 1.8 to 5.1.
- The rates are measured from 0.1 to 0.6 s after the strike, and the room
  falls at them after that too.
