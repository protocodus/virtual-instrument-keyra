# Keyra piano modelling review — 1 November 2026

> Historical research baseline: this review inspected `e96ce8a8bf58`. Implementation now starts from `ba3ee9908645`, which adds Belmont and soundboard-fitting fixes. See [the refreshed baseline and trials](../validation/realism-baseline-and-trials-2026-11-01.md).

Keyra should keep its measured modal engine as the production foundation and bring selected interaction physics into it from the existing full-model research. The highest-value work is better evidence for playing behavior, more reliable calibration between recorded notes, and a reduced persistent string/hammer/bridge system. A wholesale solver replacement is not justified by the available performance or listening evidence.

The follow-up [techniques ranked by expected audible impact](piano-techniques-audible-priorities-2026-11-01.md) compares specific algorithms with the earlier experiments and refines the first audible prototype to attack/body observation, followed by treble ringing, before integrated restrike physics.

**The production model is a measured modal synthesizer.** It reconstructs the components heard in recordings using numerical parameters, without loading recorded waveforms or impulse responses. It does not simulate all the mechanical interactions that produced those recordings.

| Stage | Current implementation | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Calibration | Numeric models fitted from five piano libraries: Kakegawa, Helsinki upright, Hamamatsu, Hamburg and New York | Several README passages still say three pianos; code and the latest decision log establish five |
| Strike | Velocity selects/interpolates measured excitation; deliberate extra velocity color and deterministic touch variation modify it | Continuous response is already present, but existing vibration does not affect hammer contact |
| Strings | Up to 128 strike voices and 128 partial slots per voice; four damped complex lanes per partial carry beating, phase, decay and stereo pickup | Detailed note reconstruction; lanes are fitted components, not necessarily four physical strings |
| Body and attack | Shared body/case modes, per-strike residual band noise and attack transients | The grand has 91 body modes; the other four have 89/38/110/79. They are fitted acoustic components, not independently measured structural modes |

Repeated strikes currently allocate independent voices. A previous note continues sounding, but its displacement/velocity cannot change the next hammer collision. Similarly, the body's one-shot excitation and the feed-forward sympathy bank cannot reproduce reciprocal loading. These are architectural limits, not proof that a more expensive solver will sound better.

The Build system mixes measured descriptions of strings, hammers, body and action. Because the source recordings already contain their combined interactions, those parts are not uniquely identified physical objects. Adding a new bridge resonance or contact model on top of the existing fitted output risks counting the same effect twice. A causal replacement needs joint refitting or explicit removal of the old contribution.

**The full physical path already contains much of the proposed physics.** `Experiments/` is a separate project excluded from the product build. Its documented model has 230 strings, two transverse polarizations, 17,778 string modes, 475 soundboard modes and three effective bridge ports. It includes nonlinear hammer contact, reciprocal coupling, persistent restrikes, recovery and mechanism/body interaction. Its contact law is dissipative Hunt–Crossley, not hereditary felt hysteresis. Its full API exposes sustain only; production's complete pedal feature set must be retained during any transfer. See [coverage inventory](../../Experiments/docs/PIANO_PARTS_COVERAGE.md), [backend budget](../../Experiments/docs/FULL_BACKEND_BUDGET.md) and [full API](../../Experiments/include/keyra/FullInstrument.h).

SIMD, fast contact and mechanism caching have already been investigated. The latest documented pressure-SIMD study still takes 2.154–2.292 CPU seconds per audio second at 48 kHz, and every measured callback misses its deadline. Those are historical shared-Linux-host results, not present Mac, Reason or production-engine measurements. An older profile makes bridge/string radiation much more expensive than contact solving. Re-profile the integrated candidate before further optimization. [Pressure study](../../Experiments/docs/PRESSURE_CURRENT.md), lines 65–85.

The waveguide prototypes are also substantial but incomplete. Bass/middle waveguides plus modal treble are a credible research option; the experiments do not yet establish a sound-equivalent 88-key instrument with detuned unisons, both polarizations, complete damping and radiation. A coupled waveguide prototype's damper is placed at the hammer position, not the real damper position. [Waveguide feasibility](../../Experiments/docs/WAVEGUIDE_FEASIBILITY.md), [coupled waveguides](../../Experiments/docs/COUPLED_WAVEGUIDE.md).

Previous negative results matter: six broad body-radiation weights passed none of 34 preservation cases; a contact-driven transient companion network failed hard C8 and altered stereo while adding another network's cost. Do not repeat either as an established shortcut. [Body radiation](../../Experiments/docs/BODY_RADIATION.md), [transient radiation](../../Experiments/docs/TRANSIENT_RADIATION.md).

**Research supports a reduced physical model, with learned calibration as a tool.** There is no single publicly established algorithm that wins simultaneously on acoustic accuracy, playing realism, CPU, controllability and data requirements.

| Research direction | Evidence and limitations | Application to Keyra |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Efficient modal/waveguide synthesis | Bank, Zambon and Fontana's real-time modal piano includes transverse/longitudinal behavior and practical aliasing/efficiency treatment. Its sympathetic coupling is deliberately one-way for stability. This remains an engineering baseline, not a claim of the newest research. [2010 paper](https://home.mit.bme.hu/~bank/publist/taslp10.pdf) | Modal synthesis itself is not the problem. Compare reduced reciprocal coupling against the already useful feed-forward baseline |
| Energy-consistent coupled physics | Chabassier's research connects hammer, nonlinear strings, bridge, soundboard and acoustic radiation, with energy-based numerical treatment. The 2019 overview explicitly distinguishes scientific simulators from efficient musical instruments. [Research and overview](https://juliettechabassier.perso.math.cnrs.fr/public/focus/2023/piano/) | Use the full engine as a behavioral reference; preserve stable mechanical interfaces when reducing it |
| Reduced soundboard and una corda | The 2024 una-corda study couples stiff strings to a reduced modal soundboard. A 2025 Southampton thesis evaluates which nonlinearities and structural detail matter; its studied cases find small hammer-shank/hysteresis effects. Those findings are model-specific. [2024 paper](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/499445/), [2025 thesis record/abstract](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/504922/) | Prioritize unison energy transfer and damper regulation before expensive detailed action geometry; test complexity against audible benefit |
| Differentiable physical/spectral fitting | The 2024 physics-informed model learns quasi-harmonic piano parameters. The 2025 sines/transient/noise extension adds separate learned components and trichord coupling, while reporting remaining attack limitations. [2024 paper](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/signal-processing/articles/10.3389/frsip.2023.1276748/full), [2025 paper record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.06513) | Make the offline fitter differentiable or jointly optimized where useful; emit compact stable parameters for the existing runtime |
| Neural state-space synthesis | Piano-SSM, DAFx 2025, demonstrates causal C++17 inference; its largest model reports 0.44 CPU seconds/audio second at 44.1 kHz on a specified i7-12700. This is not Keyra CPU or commercial-piano parity; its reported microseconds describe inference, not full device latency. [Paper](https://dafx25.dii.univpm.it/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/DAFx25_paper_57.pdf) | A useful research comparator or constrained residual model; insufficient reason to replace Keyra's interpretable controls with a neural instrument |
| Direct impact radiation | A 2026 Forum Acusticum contribution studies hammer acceleration noise and reports a generally minor contribution, potentially more noticeable near the action. This is conference evidence, not a universal audibility result. [Official program/abstract](https://indico.iem.at/event/10/timetable/?view=standard_inline_minutes) | Do not prioritize detailed impact-air simulation ahead of larger measured attack, decay and interaction gaps |

**Recommended work, in order of practical value.** The ordering below is an engineering judgment grounded in the audit, not a measured prediction of sound quality. Calibration and interaction prototypes can proceed in parallel once their evaluation cases are fixed.

| Priority | Concrete proposal | Evidence needed to advance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P0 — trustworthy reference | Complete the existing performance-capture and listening plan: synchronized audio, note timing, known pedal travel, repeated takes, release information where available; reserve a fresh session | Independent interaction cases and lettered, level-matched comparisons through the exact product path |
| P1 — calibration/generalization | Improve cross-pitch/velocity fitting with uncertainty bounds, stable decay constraints and separate source versus stereo-observation parameters; target weak treble and sparse libraries first | Per-register attack, decay, level and stereo improvements on dev cases, with every regression reported; confirm once on fresh evaluation data |
| P1 — damper regulation | Fit per-register pedal thresholds, damping and motion dependence; represent grand/upright differences explicitly | Measured half-pedal, repedal and release trajectories; preserve existing preferred voicing and saved behavior |
| P2 — persistent interaction core | Prototype persistent unison states, nonlinear contact during collisions, and a small passive reciprocal bridge/body model, using existing research components | Audible benefit in repeated notes, silent-key sympathy and pedaled chords, bounded energy and callback headroom |
| P2 — causal radiation | Fit stable shared structural poles with multiple observation outputs, spatial residues and measured relative delay | Consistent near/player/distant perspectives, mono compatibility and held-out spatial agreement |
| P3 — additional complexity | Evaluate longitudinal/phantom-partial refinement, hybrid waveguides, hereditary felt or a small learned residual only against remaining errors | A specific observable improvement that justifies CPU/data/maintenance cost |

P0 extends existing infrastructure rather than replacing it. The current recording benchmark renders isolated held notes, removes a level scalar, and reduces output gain to avoid the limiter. It is valuable for calibration but cannot establish repetition, pedal, chord or final-output dynamics. Dev cases are repeatedly consulted to select candidates, and the sealed-opening log records previous exposure of some reserved cases. Preserve that history and obtain a genuinely fresh final evaluation set. [Benchmark protocol](../../Tools/benchmark_recordings.py), [production plan](../plans/production-readiness.md), [sealed-opening record](../../Tools/corpus/sealed-openings.json).

P1 has concrete targets. The frozen Hamamatsu report shows mean dev partial-level error of 9.88 dB against 7.74 dB for the grand-data baseline, despite better tuning/noise/level measures. Hamburg's eight dev cases cover only two middle-register pitches. New York's MIDI105 decay error rises from 5.54 to 29.05 dB/s against its baseline. These are specific recording-agreement failures, not realism percentages. The latest decision record also says the two newest fitted pianos had not yet been auditioned. [Hamamatsu report](../validation/kawai-recording-benchmark-2026-10-31.md), [Hamburg report](../validation/hamburg-recording-benchmark-2026-10-31.md), [New York report](../validation/newyork-recording-benchmark-2026-10-31.md), [decisions](../decisions.md).

For calibration, model confidence should determine when to retain a prior rather than infer a component from noise, masking, gated tails or a missing velocity layer. Jointly fit pitch/velocity trends while allowing measured exceptions; do not smooth away genuine instrument irregularities. Separate excitation, mechanical decay and microphone response as far as the data permits. Multi-microphone recordings alone do not uniquely identify bridge mobility: controlled mechanical measurements or explicitly acknowledged physical priors are needed for that step.

For P2, start with a few representative bass/middle/treble notes and related receiver strings. Match bridge admittance, contact response, decay and radiation before scaling to the full keyboard. Reduce by input/output participation and energy, not simply by dropping modes above a chosen frequency. Require stable coupling under all supported control settings. Use brief oversampling around nonlinear contact only if aliasing measurements justify it. The existing fitted four lanes cannot be assumed to expose a unique physical hammer-contact displacement without a new identification model.

Keep the following owner choices intact: full sustain currently reaches 80% damper lift, releases are deliberately subtle, and velocity color/touch variation were explicitly selected. A measured full-clearance regulation could be an additional option; it should not silently replace those choices. The current API lacks release velocity and a distinct silent-key-down event. MIDI note-on velocity zero means note-off, so silent depression needs an explicit internal control and a documented way to receive it; do not pretend ordinary MIDI supplies unavailable motion information. [Engine](../../Source/DSP/KeyraEngine.cpp), lines 269–279; [API](../../Source/DSP/KeyraEngine.h), line 99; [sound decisions](../decisions.md).

For radiation, begin with a few measured perspectives, not arbitrary virtual microphone coordinates. The current Tube control is a useful color control; turning it into a physical microphone needs relative phase/delay and source-direction information. Retain the original pressure path as a comparator, and use the failed radiation experiments to guard against deleting body/string contributions that the fit still needs.

**The first implementation milestone should be narrow and reviewable.** Freeze the current product as A, assemble an interaction suite, and build one candidate that changes only persistent restrike/contact behavior on representative keys. In parallel, fix one well-localized calibration failure using only training data. Render identical MIDI/controls/seeds for A and each candidate, level-match listening material, and keep the answer key separate. Also assess live playing: loudness matching alone cannot evaluate touch response.

The suite should cover fast repetitions at several intervals, quiet-after-loud and loud-after-quiet strikes, held receiver strings, half-pedal sweeps, delayed repedaling, sostenuto, soft pedal, short releases, long tails, dense chords and stereo fold-down. Measure the final output stage separately: a good pre-limiter fit can still lose dynamic range in hard chords. Compare with Pianoteq 9 and Piano V3 using the same performance, while acknowledging that different target acoustic pianos should not be judged by spectral identity.

Measure CPU on the target Mac and actual Reason/native hosts at supported rates and block sizes, including synchronous note-on bursts and dense pedal tails. Report maximum and percentile callback times, deadline misses, memory and latency; average render speed is insufficient. Do not claim unchanged sound from a numerical null bound or better realism from a lower spectral loss. Advance only when the candidate has a clear listening/measurement benefit at an acceptable integrated cost.
