# The knock under 400 Hz

9 November 2026, Keyra 0.4.28. A hammer's blow knocks the board and the case
through the bridge, and the knock falls faster than the body's modes ring.
0.4.27 played that knock only from 400 Hz up, where `fit_realtime_modal.py`
measures it, and under it the treble's first tens of milliseconds stood under
its recordings on every piano. 0.4.28 plays the knock in five more
third-octave bands, 125 to 315 Hz, measured from each piano's train takes.

The measurement is `Tools/fit_low_knock.py`; its protocol is at the head of
the tool and its receipt is [`low-knock-2026-11-09.json`](low-knock-2026-11-09.json),
which `Source/DSP/MeasuredLowKnock.h` is generated from and
`Keyra.MeasuredLowKnock` checks. It reads the train takes of each piano's
locked split only. No take is loaded or distributed, and the knock is fitted
numbers, not a recorded waveform.

## Why the bands started cold

The noise bands are sixth-order band-passes over a white source that runs on
the engine's clock. `silenceNoise()`, which runs at `prepare()`, at `reset()`
and when the engine falls silent, set their filters to rest, and a band-pass
from rest takes about the inverse of its bandwidth to reach the power its
source holds it at: at 125 Hz a band stood 10 to 18 dB under its stationary
power 20 to 30 ms in, and at 198 Hz about 10 dB under 15 to 20 ms in. So the
first strike after a silence knocked more quietly than a strike in the middle
of playing, and the benchmarks, which render every note from silence, heard
the cold one.

The five new bands start from their stationary state. At each rate
`prepareNoiseStates()` works out the covariance the source leaves in a band's
filter state (its three sections' inputs and outputs, through 24 doublings of
the state's transition) and keeps its Cholesky factor; `silenceNoise()` draws
each band's state from it with a fixed seed. Every silence then leaves the
bands in the same state, so a strike after a second of silence and after ten
sounds the same, and the knock is at its band's full power from contact.
`Keyra.LowKnock` checks the factor against the filters' impulse responses at
44.1, 48 and 96 kHz, and the state after sleep and after `reset()`.

The measured bands from 400 Hz up keep the rest start. Started from their
stationary state too, as a trial, their first 10 ms stood 2 to 9 dB over the
recordings: `fit_realtime_modal.py` measures their knock from contact with
no rise, against the cold start. They can change only with a rise for that
knock.

## The knock

Per band the knock is the band's noise under an envelope
A (e^(−rt) − e^(−(r + 1/τ)t)) from contact, the rise form an action's click
takes: a decay rate r and a rise τ per piano and band, a density per recorded
pitch as a line in the level the strike's layer plays at, and the band's
left/right balance and coherence. Between two recorded pitches a key mixes
their levels in dB; where only one of them measures a band, the key plays it
as far as it lies toward that one. Between pianos the knocks mix in power by
the soundboard's shares, their rates and rises in the log domain. The top
keys' noise trim applies as it does to the noise bands. A band is measured at
a pitch only where its upper edge lies a third of an octave under the
fundamental, so the lowest band from F♯3 up and all five from A♯4 up; a key
whose recorded pitches either side measure none plays none.

The knock reaches the room, as the strike's noise does: the room hears the
mid of what the noise bands play.

## Measuring it

Each take and its render through the engine without this knock
(`--no-low-knock`), aligned and scaled as above, pass through the engine's
band filter in 5 ms frames from the strike to 0.5 s after it, the
benchmark's attack window (to 0.1 s) and its early window (to 0.5 s), which
the fit weighs alike. The knock's expected power per frame, through the
band's filters, under the envelope and through the filters again to measure
it, is worked out exactly from the filters' joint state for every rate of 20
to 2000 dB/s and rise of 0.5 to 40 ms on the fit's grids. To it the fit adds
what the room returns of it: the room's sixteen lines, fed alike at the room's
gain over 4, hold the input's energy and give it back at their mean length's
share per sample, losing it at the room's rate at the band from the shortest
line's delay on. Against the engine's own echo of a knock, the difference of
renders with the room on and off, that model holds within 1.1 dB over 0.1 to
1.4 s in every band, here on the Hamburg grand's D♯6 at velocity 90 (dB,
summed power over 0.1 to 1.4 s):

| band | 125 Hz | 157 Hz | 198 Hz | 250 Hz | 315 Hz |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| the engine's echo | −78.9 | −69.5 | −74.4 | −73.1 | −67.8 |
| the model's | −78.3 | −70.6 | −74.3 | −73.1 | −68.6 |

How much of the knock the room hears follows from the band's balance and
coherence, measured first, through the frames of the first 0.1 s where the
recording stands 3 dB over its render. The fit compares the recording's
power with the render's plus the knock's in dB, each held up to 30 dB under
the recording's loudest frame, so a knock that would raise frames the render
already fills, or frames the recording leaves quiet, is fitted away. The
shape is the decay and rise that fit a piano's takes in the band best
together, each take at its own best level; the level per pitch is the line,
of slope 0 to 2 dB per dB of layer level, that fits its takes best under that
shape, where three takes or more clear the band.

"Room hears" is the mid's power over the channels' mean. The frame error is
the fitted pitches' mean over their takes, the two windows weighed alike. Most
rises sit at the grid's fastest, 0.5 ms: the knock is at full power as soon as
the band's filter lets it be. The upright's three lowest bands fall at 32 to
43 dB/s and the Kakegawa grand's two lowest at 50 and 80 dB/s, about as slowly
as their rooms there (33 and 56 dB/s): on the upright the dev takes, never
fitted, stand 0.25 dB closer early and 0.10 dB in the sustain with them.

## On the benchmark

A first fit measured the knock over the first 0.1 s only and left the room
out. It brought four pianos closer and the Hamburg further, on train and dev:
over its first 0.1 s a knock decaying at 37 to 50 dB/s fitted the Hamburg's
takes, and on to 3 s it and the Hamburg's room, the slowest of the five
(16 dB/s at low frequencies, at the highest gain), stood over its recordings.

level in tolerance units:

## What it does not do

- From 400 Hz up the treble still stands under its recordings through the
  first 30 ms: at 400 Hz by 3.8 dB on the Kakegawa grand, 6.6 on the
  Hamamatsu, 5.1 on the Hamburg and 9.1 on New York. Those bands are
  `fit_realtime_modal.py`'s knock, fitted from contact with no rise, which
  start from rest after a silence and stationary once the source has run
  through them: their first strike after a silence is the quieter one. A rise
  for that knock and a stationary start, measured together, would close it.
- Under 125 Hz nothing changed: 30 to 60 ms in, the Hamburg's treble stands 8
  to 10 dB under its recordings from 50 to 80 Hz.
- Between sparse recorded pitches the knock's level is a mix of theirs, and
  where the engine already stands over a recording there, as at the
  Hamburg's C♯5, the knock adds to the excess.
- No string keeps the knock up, so a mute or a key let go leaves it ringing
  at its own decay, as the knock above 400 Hz and the action's knocks do:
  from 0.2 to 0.7 s after a staccato C5 is let go, it and the room's echo of
  it stand 44 to 68 dB under the note at the product's defaults (the
  Hamburg's, with its slow room, the loudest). Whether a real board's knock
  stops sooner under the dampers is not measured.
- The rises at the grid's edges (0.5 ms on most bands, 40 ms on two) say the
  fit wanted faster or slower ones than the grid holds.
