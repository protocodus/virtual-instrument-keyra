# The struck key's own undamped string (2026-11-05)

The strings from F6 up have no dampers. The engine plays each as one tone at
its measured pitch that every strike sets ringing, at the level the recorded
pitches around the struck key set it, except the struck key's own: its
partials were taken to play it. Read against the recordings, they do
not wherever none of the key's own components comes near the string's pitch,
and there the key's own strike held a tone the engine left out. 0.4.24 plays
it. `Tools/fit_halo_own.py` measures it on each piano's train takes and writes
`Source/DSP/MeasuredHaloOwn.h`; its receipt is
[halo-own-2026-11-05.json](halo-own-2026-11-05.json).

## What the recordings hold

The grand's F♯7 strikes hold a tone 8.6 Hz under their fundamental, from −2 to
+2 dB against it from 1 to 2 s after contact, and its A7 strikes one 28 Hz
under, from −15 to −5 dB, where the halo fit measured those keys' strings in
the other keys' strikes (3003.50 and 3571.75 Hz). From F7 up the string an
octave below is undamped too, and the strike sets it ringing through the
bridge at its second partial: A7's tone lies within 0.1 Hz of A6's second
partial as A6's fit gives it (3571.84 Hz). Below F7 the string an octave down
is damped, and the tone can only be a string of the key's own unison tuned
further off than its partials' lanes reach (the Hamamatsu grand's A6, the
Hamburg's A♯6, the New York grand's F6).

The engine played neither. Read as the halo fit reads a string, through
half-second windows from 1 to 2 s, brought back to contact at the string's
rate and taken against the take's level from 50 to 750 ms, 0.4.23's renders
held 30 to 43 dB less than the recordings at F♯7 and 59 to 64 dB less at A7.

## How it is measured

A recorded pitch's own string is read where its fitted series leaves the
string's pitch apart: no lane of any of its partials within 4 Hz of it, the
main lobe of the halo fit's half-second Hann window, inside which that window
resolves no second component. Each train take of such a pitch is read through
the halo fit's windows (`halo_tone`, the strongest bin within 1 Hz of the
pitch), and a window hears the string clear where its tone stands 10 dB over

- the median from 4 to 20 Hz either side, leaving out the bins within 4 Hz of
  the key's own components, and
- what those components alone read there through the same window: each lane
  of the key's fitted series within 40 Hz of the string, at the take's layer
  and falling at its own rate, its powers summed.

The second test decides next to a fundamental. On the Hamamatsu grand's C7 the
string lies 4.2 Hz from the fundamental, and the reading there was as loud in
the recording as in the engine's render, which plays no own string: it was the
fundamental's first sidelobe in both. It never stands over the predicted
leakage, so C7 bounds nothing and plays no own string.

As the halo fit has it, a layer's level is the mean of its clear windows,
brought back to contact, where two or more ring clear; otherwise its 1 s
window bounds it, where that stands over the key's own components. The string
grows with the strike at the halo fit's slope (1.48 dB per dB of the layer's
level on the grand, plus its rise per octave, nil or nearly here, as the
string lies at the key's own pitch). A pitch's level at its loudest layer is fitted
over its layers, the hidden ones as bounds (a censored fit with one spread);
where fewer than two ring clear it sits 10 dB under their median bound, never
over the tightest, a layer that rings clear bounding it at its level. The
Hamburg's F7 rings clear only at its softest layer, which puts its loud level
18 dB under where its loud layers' bounds alone put it (−99.0 against −80.7
dB). A recorded pitch the series fit leaves uncalibrated has no fitted series
to tell its own components from the string, so its layers only bound it, at
their 1 s window: the grand's C8, whose strongest components lie 90 to 100
cents over the stretch trend, holds a tone at its string's pitch, 140 Hz and
more from them, 39 to 46 dB under the take.

Between the recorded pitches each key takes the levels of the pitches around
it, interpolated by key where both have one and held from the one that does
otherwise; a key between two pitches whose own components play their strings
has none, as has a key past such a pitch at the ends.

## In the engine

`strike()` now sets the struck key's own string ringing too, on each piano
whose table gives it a level and whose series, as the engine builds it for
that key, leaves the string's pitch apart: `ownStringApart()` takes every lane
of every partial up to twice the string's pitch, at the key's built pitch and
stiffness, and finds none within `haloOwnResolvedHz` (4 Hz). So the Kakegawa
grand's D♯7, F7 and F♯7 to C8 ring their own strings, the Hamamatsu grand's F6,
A6, B6, C♯7, D♯7, F7, F♯7 and A7 to B7, the Hamburg's F♯6, A6 to B6, C♯7, D7
and E7 to A♯7, and the New York grand's F6, G♯6 and E7 to C8; the upright's
none. The level is the table's, grown with the strike's layer at the undamped
strings' slope, the same aftersound every other key's strike sets on that string, with its beat
where the string beats. It has no prompt part: the prompt part is a struck
unison's, and this string is not struck. Between pianos it plays at the share
of the hammers of the pianos that ring it, in power, as a partial only some
pianos have does. Its ringing goes into the string's one resonator, so it
rings on after the key is let go, the felt mute damps it, and a strike adds no
per-sample work (struck chromatically with the pedal down, the four pianos it
changes render within the spread of seven alternating runs against 0.4.23's,
from −3.5 to +1.7 %); `Keyra.HaloOwn` checks the level against the table on every
piano, the gate, the silence where the table gives none, the share between
pianos and that the host's blocks do not change it.

## Each pitch's own slope (0.4.26)

0.4.24 grew every own string with the strike at the halo fit's slope, one per
piano. Where three or more of a pitch's train layers ring its string clear
(`SLOPED`), 0.4.26 fits the pitch's own slope with its level in the same
censored fit, between 0 and 3 dB per dB (`SLOPES`), and `haloOwnSlopeDb`
carries how far it lies from the halo fit's. The keys between take that
change from the pitches around them as they take the levels. Four pitches
ring clear in three layers or more; the Hamamatsu grand's two ring in two

## What it leaves

- Four pitches grow at their own slopes and every other own string at the
  halo fit's. A key between takes the slopes of the pitches around it, as it
  takes their levels: the grand's D♯7, which holds F♯7's string, stands
  further from its dev takes with F♯7's slope than with the halo fit's.
- Keys between two recorded pitches take the levels around them. The New York
  grand's A♯6 to D♯7 lie between two pitches whose partials play their
  strings and have none; from E7 up its keys hold G♯7's bound, which leaves
  the never-fitted E7, whose takes hold the tone 9 dB under the take, 32 dB
  under them, and A7 20 dB under. The Hamamatsu's D♯7, bounded far under,
  pulls the keys interpolated towards it down: its F♯7 sits 17 to 24 dB under.
- The own string plays at the pitch the other keys' strikes measure for the
  string. At A7 that is where A6's second partial rings. At F♯7 the strikes
  ring F♯6's second partial at 3001.1 Hz, where F♯6's fit puts it (3001.2
  Hz), 2.4 Hz under the 3003.5 Hz the other keys' strikes give; through a
  window from 50 ms to 1.5 s it stands 0 to 2 dB under F♯7's fundamental,
  where the render's own string stands 9 dB under it, though read from 1 to
  2 s as the fit reads it the two agree within 4 dB. Playing a key's own
  string from F7 up at the octave-below string's second partial, as that
  string's fit gives it, would place it; it takes a resonator per string
  beside the one the other keys' strikes ring.
